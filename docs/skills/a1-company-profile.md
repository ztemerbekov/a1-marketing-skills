# A1 Company Profile

**English** | [Русский](./a1-company-profile.ru.md)

Use A1 Company Profile to structure or draft one factual public company profile or About page.

## The job

The skill answers the primary audience's real questions about what the company does, for whom, how it works, why it is credible, and what the reader should do next. It turns supplied company facts, history, operating practices, policies, and evidence into a coherent public narrative.

## When to use it

Ask for an outline when you need the reader questions, answers, and evidence map. Ask for a draft when the confirmed inputs are sufficient for publishable prose.

The skill does not invent positioning, mission, values, history, credentials, social impact, or culture. It uses mission or values only when confirmed decisions and observable practices support them. A page built around one offer belongs to A1 Promo Page; a news event belongs to A1 Press Release.

## What you get

An outline maps the audience's questions to supported answers and evidence. A draft returns the profile in publication order and names only material source gaps that prevent completion.

> **Try asking:**
>
> `/a1-company-profile`
>
> Draft an About page for procurement leaders using the attached company history, service model, security policy, office list, and approved customer evidence. Do not infer a mission or values.

## Common questions

**Can it write our mission and values?**

Only from confirmed decisions and practices. It does not turn an aspiration or a generic adjective into a current company claim.

**Can one profile serve every audience?**

The skill chooses one primary audience and public context. Other readers may still understand it, but the structure follows the primary audience's questions.

**Can it write a founder biography?**

Only when that biography is supporting evidence inside the company profile. A standalone personal profile is a different format.

## How to recognize success

- The opening quickly explains what the company does and for whom.
- Credibility comes from specific facts and practices rather than unsupported adjectives.
- History appears only where it helps the reader judge the company today.
- Mission and values are connected to observable decisions or behavior.
- The profile ends with a relevant, operational next step.
