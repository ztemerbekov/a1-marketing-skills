# A1 Decision Memo

**English** | [Русский](./a1-decision-memo.ru.md)

Use A1 Decision Memo when one decision-maker needs to understand a situation, decide, approve, respond, or stay informed without reading the entire source trail.

## The job

You have reports, correspondence, notes, analysis, chronology, or meeting material. You want one internal document that puts the decision or status first and keeps enough detail for the reader to verify it.

The skill organizes completed specialist input. It does not redo legal, financial, technical, or strategic analysis, and it does not invent an option, recommendation, owner, deadline, or approval.

## When to use it

Use it for an approval memo, executive brief, project-status memo, recommendation memo, escalation note, or awareness-only update. The skill can start from an unambiguous natural-language request or through `/a1-decision-memo`.

Choose [A1 Business Message](./a1-business-message.md) for one compact send-ready messenger or ordinary business-email message for an existing business contact; keep Decision Memo for a standalone layered, reference-ready internal decision, response, or awareness/status document whose supporting detail, including chronology as applicable, must remain inspectable, even when delivered by email. The communication goal alone does not choose the owner; artifact shape does. If both shapes remain materially plausible and the choice would materially change depth or structure, ask which artifact shape the user wants.

Use [A1 Copy Editing](./a1-copy-editing.md) when a selected finished memo only needs editing or review. Use [A1 Article Writing](./a1-article-writing.md) for a public or educational long-form piece.

## What you get

The memo opens with a self-contained executive layer: what happened, why it matters, and whether the reader must decide, approve, respond, or do nothing. The rest of the document adds options and trade-offs when supplied, risks, unknowns, next steps, and supporting detail in the order the reader needs.

Marketing Context may provide confirmed defaults, but it never replaces the memo's source material.

## Common questions

**Will it choose an option for me?**

Only when the supplied recommendation or decision criteria support that conclusion. Otherwise the memo shows the options and names the unresolved criterion.

**Does the memo have to be short?**

No. It must be layered. The top line should stand on its own while the detail remains available for verification.

**Can it organize legal or financial material?**

Yes, when that analysis is supplied as completed source material. The skill preserves specialist meaning and attribution but does not replace professional judgment or approval.

## How to recognize success

- The opening makes the situation and required response clear.
- Facts, assessments, options, recommendations, and confirmed decisions cannot be confused.
- Each section begins with its significance for the reader.
- Chronology and technical detail remain available without controlling the reading path.
- Missing criteria, owners, dates, or approvals stay visible.
