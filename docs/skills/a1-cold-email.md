# A1 Cold Email

**English** | [Русский](./a1-cold-email.ru.md)

Use A1 Cold Email to write one credible first-contact email to one identifiable recipient.

## The job

The skill turns supplied recipient, relationship, sender, offer, and proof facts into one personalized cold email. It establishes why this recipient should hear from this sender now, makes a bounded value case, and asks for a low-friction next step.

## When to use it

Use it when no prior conversation exists and you need one new outreach email. Supply a recipient dossier or completed prospect research, the offer, relevant proof, and any sender context that makes the contact legitimate.

It does not research prospects, create bulk templates or sequences, continue an existing conversation, or edit a selected draft.

## What you get

One subject line and one finished email, usually in two to five short paragraphs. Material gaps are listed only when they prevent a credible message. The skill never invents familiarity, referrals, compliments, pain points, credentials, results, urgency, or scarcity.

> **Try asking:**
>
> `/a1-cold-email`
>
> Write one cold email to the COO of Northwind Logistics. Use the attached recipient dossier, our implementation offer, and the two confirmed customer results. Ask whether a 15-minute fit check would be useful.

## Common questions

**Can it write a three-email sequence?**

No. Its unit of work is one first-contact email to one recipient.

**Will it look up the recipient?**

No. Prospect research is a completed input. The skill writes only from supplied or already selected evidence.

**What if the fit is uncertain?**

It keeps the uncertainty visible and writes a proportionate question or stops for the missing fact. It does not convert a guess into personalization.

## How to recognize success

- The message explains why the contact is relevant without fake familiarity.
- The offer and proof match the recipient's known context.
- The email is short, specific, and easy to answer.
- Every personal claim and result is source-supported.
- The requested next step is proportionate to a first contact.
