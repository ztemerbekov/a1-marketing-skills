# Evidence ledger

Evidence grades are operational confidence labels for this skill: **A** has strong synthesis and/or robust foundational evidence; **B** has useful evidence with meaningful limits or heterogeneous synthesis; **C** is conceptually relevant but especially contested or context-sensitive. None implies a local causal result or a promised effect size.

## M06 — Sunk-Cost Effect (B)

- Arkes & Blumer (1985), foundational field and questionnaire experiments, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0749-5978(85)90049-4).
- Roth, Robbert & Straus (2015), meta-analytic review of 98 effect sizes, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40685-014-0014-8).
- Petrov et al. (2023), registered-report replication and extensions, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5334/irsp.883).

Prior investment must be irrecoverable and future value/friction must be separated. Evidence is heterogeneous across utilization and progress decisions; waste avoidance, self-justification, regret, reputation, and mental accounting remain competing explanations. Do not infer a local retention effect or treat loss aversion, endowment, contracts, or mere progress as sunk cost.

## M08 — IKEA Effect (B)

- Norton, Mochon & Ariely (2012), successful self-assembly experiments, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2011.08.002).
- Sarstedt, Neubert & Barth (2017), conceptual replication, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1561/107.00000039).
- Pelled, Demetriades & Walter (2025), meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/mar.70064).

The evidence concerns successful self-production and valuation, not generic customization or ownership. Completion, tangibility, outcome type, ownership, and selection matter; broad real-purchase, return, and retention effects remain uncertain.

## M14 — Goal-Gradient Effect (B)

- Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng (2006), loyalty and experimental evidence on progress and purchase acceleration, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39).
- Devine et al. (2024), reward proximity and effortful control, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0001561).

Human goal-gradient evidence does not provide a broad pooled field estimate. Visible progress, reward credibility, completer selection, deadlines, habit, and reward expectancy can change the observed slope; post-reward retention is a separate outcome.

## M11 — Status-quo bias (B)

- Samuelson & Zeckhauser (1988), *Status quo bias in decision making*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00055564).
- Xiao et al. (2021), direct replication, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.15626/MP.2020.2470).
- Godefroid et al. (2023), measurement review, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11301-022-00283-8).

Use only with a genuine current-state comparison; nearby mechanisms often confound it.

## M12 — Default effect (A)

- Jachimowicz et al. (2019), meta-analysis across 58 datasets / 55 studies / 35 articles, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/bpp.2018.43).

The meta-analysis reports a positive average default effect with high heterogeneity. Do not convert its aggregate estimate into a local forecast.

## M13 — Choice overload (B)

- Scheibehenne, Greifeneder & Todd (2010), meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/651235).
- Chernev, Böckenholt & Goodman (2015), meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2014.08.002), with [corrigendum](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2015.07.001).

There is no universal threshold; the 2010 synthesis found a near-zero average effect amid high heterogeneity.

## N01 — Implementation Intentions (B)

- Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006), historical meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1).
- Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer (2024), bias-sensitive synthesis of 642 tests, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563).

The recent synthesis reports a smaller bias-adjusted average than historical estimates and substantial heterogeneity. It supports an if–then cue–response plan only when a meaningful goal, feasible cue/action, and contingent response are present; it is not evidence that reminders, schedules, or general planning reliably cause behavior.

## N03 — Regulatory Focus Theory (B)

- Higgins (1997), foundational theory, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.52.12.1280).
- Lanaj, Chang & Johnson (2012), work-outcomes meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0027723).
- Fuglestad et al. (2025), multi-sample measure validation, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jopy.12962).

Measure, induction, valence, personality, and risk preference can overlap. No general consumer conversion estimate follows from a promotion/prevention label; treat focus as a measured or cleanly induced orientation, not message wording alone.

## N04 — Regulatory Fit (B)

- Higgins (2000), value-from-fit account, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.11.1217).
- Motyka et al. (2014), meta-analytic synthesis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2013.11.004).

Fit is an actual focus-by-pursuit-strategy interaction, not a single “matched” treatment or synonym for regulatory focus. Its direction and magnitude vary with focus source, strategy, valence, action versus observation, and outcome; test the interaction locally.

## N22 — Habit / Cue–Response Automaticity (A)

- Wood & Rünger (2016), review of habit mechanisms, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033417).
- Gardner, Rebar & Lally (2023), meta-analysis of habit, behavior, and intention relations, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/mot0000294).
- Ma et al. (2023), habit-formation intervention meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-023-01493-3).
- Singh et al. (2024), systematic review and meta-analysis of formation time, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare12232488).

Automaticity cannot be inferred from frequency, loyalty, streaks, or retention. Measures and formation conditions vary, and formation time has a wide individual range; do not claim a universal 21- or 66-day rule or infer compulsion-free retention from repeated behavior.

## N27 — Psychological Reactance Theory (B)

- Rains (2013), meta-analytic review of reactance measurement, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2958.2012.01443.x).
- Steindl et al. (2015), integrative review, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1027/2151-2604/a000222).

Reactance requires a perceived threat to a valued freedom and is commonly measured through linked anger and counterarguing. State/trait distinctions, freedom importance, source legitimacy, culture, and alternatives limit transportability; disagreement or churn alone does not establish the mechanism.

## N29 — Expectancy–Disconfirmation Model (B)

- Oliver (1980), foundational expectation and satisfaction model, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/002224378001700405).
- Schiebler, Lee & Brodbeck (2025), meta-analysis of 150 records, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-024-01078-x).

Expectation, performance, disconfirmation, satisfaction, and retention are distinct constructs. The recent synthesis is largely correlational, includes discriminant-validity concerns, and does not establish a causal retention or welfare effect; never use satisfaction as a proxy for retention.

## M30 — Loss aversion (C)

- Kahneman & Tversky (1979), prospect theory, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185).
- Ruggeri et al. (2020), large-scale evidence, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-020-0886-x).
- Brown et al. (2024), review, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.20221698).

Avoid fixed “losses are twice gains” claims; magnitude and interpretation remain contested.

## M31 — Anchoring (A)

- Tversky & Kahneman (1974), foundational study, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124).
- Schley & Weingarten (2026), meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.03238).
- Simonson & Drolet (2004), consumer willingness-to-pay, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/425103).

Evidence supports anchoring, while estimates vary greatly and arbitrary anchors can be weaker.

## M32 — Decoy effect (B)

- Huber, Payne & Puto (1982), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/208899).
- Dumbalska et al. (2020), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2005058117).
- Trendl et al. (2021), naturalistic zero-effect study, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/dec0000145).

Transport to ordinary product settings is uncertain; verify asymmetric dominance and test locally.

## M33 — Framing (A)

- Tversky & Kahneman (1981), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7455683).
- Kühberger (1998), meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1006/obhd.1998.2781).

Framing effects have documented boundary conditions; maintain factual equivalence and check comprehension.

## M02 — Mere Exposure Effect (A)

- Zajonc (1968), foundational experiments, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0025848).
- Bornstein (1989), meta-analysis of 208 experiments, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.106.2.265).
- Montoya et al. (2017), meta-analysis of 268 exposure curves from 81 articles, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000085).

Evidence supports a moderated, nonlinear exposure–liking pattern. Recognition, familiarity, liking, purchase, and retention are distinct outcomes; dose, modality, and wear-out constrain local inference.

## M03 — Availability Heuristic (B)

- Tversky & Kahneman (1973), foundational retrievability account, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(73)90033-9).
- Schwarz et al. (1991), retrieval-ease experiments, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.61.2.195).
- Weingarten & Hutchinson (2018), meta-analysis of 263 studies and 582 effects, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000122).
- Folkes (1988), consumer perceived-risk experiments, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/209141).

Evidence is strongest for specific retrieval-ease and judgment paradigms. It does not let vividness or salience alone establish the operation, and laboratory judgments need local transport testing.

## M04 — Confirmation Bias (B)

- Nickerson (1998), taxonomy review, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/1089-2680.2.2.175).
- Klayman & Ha (1987), positive-test strategy boundary, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.94.2.211).
- Hart et al. (2009), meta-analysis of 91 studies, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0015701).

Selective exposure has evidence with moderators, but it is one subcomponent. The umbrella label requires a measured prior belief and a specified evidence-selection, search, or interpretation operation.

## N08 — Processing Fluency (B)

- Alter & Oppenheimer (2009), review, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868309341564).
- Unkelbach (2007), learned reversal of the fluency–truth relation, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.33.1.219).

Fluency effects depend on subtype, attribution, learned diagnosticity, and actual legibility. Ease is not a universal signal of truth, quality, or persuasion; a laboratory judgment is not a purchase forecast.

## N20 — Affect Heuristic (B)

- Finucane et al. (2000), risk–benefit judgments, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1099-0771(200001/03)13:1%3C1::AID-BDM333%3E3.0.CO;2-S).
- Slovic et al. (2007), review, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2005.04.006).
- Efendić et al. (2022), replication and extension, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/19485506211056761).

The evidence concerns affective risk–benefit judgment. Integral affect, incidental mood, and discrete emotions require separate operations; do not transfer a valence judgment directly to purchase or retention.

## M15 — Peak–End Effect (B)

- Kahneman et al. (1993), retrospective episode-choice experiment, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1993.tb00589.x).
- Alaybek et al. (2022), meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2022.104149), with [corrigendum](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2023.104278).

The corrected synthesis remains heterogeneous. Peak–end and duration neglect concern retrospective evaluation under particular designs; average experience can also predict outcomes, and experience measures do not establish purchase or retention effects.

## M18 — Curse of Knowledge (B)

- Camerer, Loewenstein & Weber (1989), economic experiments, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/261651).
- Hinds (1999), expert prediction of novice performance, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/1076-898X.5.2.205).
- Damen et al. (2021), feedback and perspective-taking, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1747021820987080).

Evidence supports privileged-knowledge prediction error in particular task families, but there is no dedicated meta-analysis for the broad construct. Expert–novice asymmetry and prediction-versus-observed-novice performance are required for a local diagnosis.

## Pricing and valuation

These cards are distinct from each other and from loss aversion. Their grades
describe the evidence base, not a predicted local revenue effect. A pricing
diagnosis needs matching manipulation/reference, experienced payment or choice,
outcome, and horizon.

## M07 — Endowment effect (B)

- Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler (1990), controlled market experiments,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/261737).
- Plott & Zeiler (2005), procedure-control evidence,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/0002828054201387).
- Ericson & Fuster (2014), review,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-economics-080213-041320).
- Tunçel & Hammitt (2014), meta-analysis of WTP/WTA disparity,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeem.2014.06.001).

Ownership/entitlement is the defining manipulation; a WTP/WTA gap is not
unique proof and loss aversion is a possible mechanism, not an alias. Results
vary with procedures, market experience, good type, attachment, and stakes.

## M10 — Present bias / temporal discounting (A)

- Cohen et al. (2020), measurement review,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.20191074).
- Imai, Rutter & Camerer (2021), convex-time-budget meta-analysis,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/ej/ueaa115).
- Cheung, Tymula & Wang (2026), quasi-hyperbolic-discounting meta-analysis,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.04003).
- Augenblick, Niederle & Sprenger (2015), longitudinal real-effort study,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjv020).

Present bias is extra weight for immediacy and dynamic inconsistency, not every
delay preference. Estimates differ by money, effort, consumption, elicitation,
and model; monetary inconsistency can be weak. Do not infer it from deferred
billing alone.

## M19 — Mental accounting (A)

- Thaler (1999), framework review,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1099-0771(199909)12:3%3C183::AID-BDM318%3E3.0.CO;2-F).
- Abeler & Marklein (2017), labels and fungibility,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jeea/jvw007).
- Li & Feldman (2025), replication Registered Report,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.250979).
- Priolo et al. (2026), 21-country replication,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucag022).

Mental accounting requires an account, budget, label, bracketing, or
transaction-utility operation; it is not framing, sunk cost, pain of paying, or
loss aversion. Classic results include mixed outcomes and vary by scenario and
income/country context.

## N15 — Reference price (B)

- Mazumdar, Raj & Sinha (2005), integrative review,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkg.2005.69.4.84).

This is an internal remembered/expected or external displayed comparison
standard, not any crossed-out price or a generic anchor. Internal/external
formation, price history, promotion frequency, expertise, credibility, and
adaptation are material limits; a modern pooled causal effect is not claimed.

## N16 — Pain of paying (B)

- Reshadi & Fitzgerald (2023), review and research agenda,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/mar.21825).
- Schomburgk, Belli & Hoffmann (2024), cashless-effect meta-analysis,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretai.2024.05.003).

The latter finds a small, heterogeneous cashless association (392 effects;
`g=.135 [.068, .203]`, `I²=86.20%`), but payment-method effects do not identify
pain as mediator: convenience, memory, selection, and transaction context
compete. Require temporally prior affect/awareness evidence.

## M09 — Zero-price effect (B)

- Shampanier, Mazar & Ariely (2007), foundational experiments,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.1060.0254).
- Fan, Cai & Bodenhausen (2022), boomerang evidence,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-022-00842-1).
- Zhang, Lane & Grisolía (2026), norms and field evidence,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/eec.2026.10045).

The required discontinuity is from a very-low positive **total relevant** price
to true zero. Incidental costs and norms can reverse it, and uptake can move
oppositely to quantity or later quality; no pooled local revenue estimate or
retention guarantee follows.

## M35 — Charm pricing / left-digit effect (B)

- Thomas & Morwitz (2005), left-digit price cognition,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/429600).
- Troll et al. (2024), preregistered meta-analysis of just-below versus round
  prices, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcpy.1353).
- Escher et al. (2026), preregistered high-powered experiments,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/frbhe.2026.1828446).

Left-digit crossing is narrower than any 9-ending. The meta-analysis reports a
small purchase effect with substantial heterogeneity and bias-sensitive
conclusions; perceived quality was null in that synthesis. Perception or a
price ending does not guarantee demand, margin, or revenue.

## N17 — Price–quality inference (B)

- Rao & Monroe (1989), meta-analysis of price and perceived quality,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/002224378902600309).
- Völckner & Hofmann (2007), synthesis of price–quality research,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11002-007-9013-2).

Price may act as a quality cue under uncertainty; it is not evidence that
charging more improves actual quality, demand, or revenue. Category, expertise,
intrinsic diagnostic information, product familiarity, geography, design, and
era moderate the inference; no local effect size is claimed.

## Support positions

Support positions are not local causal forecasts. `U` means no single effect grade applies because the position is a regularity, taxonomy, intervention dimension, or formal framework rather than one intervention mechanism.

- **M01 Correspondence Bias / FAE (B):** Gilbert & Malone (1995), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.117.1.21). Attribution error is distinct from situation-sensitive explanation.
- **M23 Commitment and Consistency (B):** Lokhorst et al. (2013), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0013916511411477); Baca-Motes et al. (2013), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/667226). Operations are heterogeneous and should not be collapsed.
- **M37 Hick–Hyman Law (B):** Hick (1952), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470215208416600); Proctor & Schneider (2018), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2017.1322622). Evidence concerns constrained choice reaction time, not conversion.
- **M38 Nudge / Choice Architecture (B):** Mertens et al. (2022), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2107346118), with [correction](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2204059119); DellaVigna & Linos (2022), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3982/ecta18709). Heterogeneous intervention class; publication-bias and scale concerns remain.
- **M41 COM-B (B):** Michie et al. (2011), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-6-42); Keyworth et al. (2020), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjhp.12417). Framework and measures are not causal intervention proof.
- **M42 Survivorship Bias (A):** Mangel & Samaniego (1984), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1984.10478038); Hernán et al. (2004), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.ede.0000135174.63482.43); Elwert & Winship (2014), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-soc-071913-043455). Selection/collider structure determines the bias.
- **N02 Intention–Behavior Gap (U):** Webb & Sheeran (2006), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.2.249). It describes incomplete translation, not a mechanism.
- **N14 Reference-Dependent Choice (U):** Kahneman & Tversky (1979), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185). Theory family; use narrower reference operations.
- **N21 Implementation Friction (U):** intervention dimension, intentionally without one traceable effect source because time, search, money, motor, uncertainty, and cognitive-cost frictions have distinct operations and evidence bases. Name the subtype and cite its active model or direct evidence; never report a generic friction effect.
- **N23 Signaling Theory (B):** Spence (1973), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/1882010); Connelly et al. (2010), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206310388419). Separating conditions and mimicry costs are often untested locally.
- **N25 Theory of Planned Behavior (B):** Ajzen (1991), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0749-5978(91)90020-T); Armitage & Conner (2001), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1348/014466601164939). Prediction does not establish intervention causality.
- **N26 Elaboration Likelihood Model (B):** Carpenter (2014), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/hcre.12054); Kitchen et al. (2014), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1108/ejm-12-2011-0776). Processing routes and behavior transfer require direct measurement.

## N11 — Social Identity (B)

- Oyserman (2009), *Identity-based motivation*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0013740).
- Reed et al. (2012), identity-based consumer behavior framework, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijresmar.2012.08.002).
- Balliet, Wu & De Dreu (2014), in-group cooperation meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037737).

The cooperation synthesis supports a group-contingent behavioral pattern, not a unique identity mechanism in a local marketing outcome. Group meaning, interdependence, status, and boundary permeability matter; do not infer or manufacture identity.

## M21 — Bandwagon cues / descriptive norms (B)

- Schultz et al. (2007), descriptive-norm boomerang field experiment, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01917.x).
- Melnyk, van Herpen & Trijp (2013), consumer social-norm meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1027/2151-2604/a000352).
- Wang, Chu & Huang (2023), bandwagon-cue credibility meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/00936502221124395).
- Papakonstantinou et al. (2025), social-norms messaging review and meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02275-6).

Effects vary by norm type, reference group/source, baseline behavior, cost, culture, and public/private context. The health synthesis was strongly publication-bias-sensitive; a generic popularity claim is neither a local forecast nor evidence of quality or approval.

## M22 — Reciprocity Principle (B)

- Gouldner (1960), *The Norm of Reciprocity*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/2092623).
- Falk (2007), gift-exchange field experiment, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2007.00800.x).
- Chuan, Kessler & Milkman (2018), timing of reciprocity in charitable giving, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1708293115).

There is no single modern meta-analysis of direct reciprocity in persuasion. Favor, gift exchange, reciprocal concession, gratitude, liking, and a hidden obligation are not interchangeable; timing and the actual behavioral outcome limit transportability.

## M24 — Authority / source credibility (B)

- Hamer et al. (2026), credible-source behavioral systematic review and meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/01461672261445474).
- Wilson & Sherrell (1993), source-effects meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/009207039302100202).
- Ismagilova et al. (2020), source-credibility characteristics and consumer behavior meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretconser.2019.01.005).
- Knoll & Matthes (2017), celebrity-endorsement meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-016-0503-8).

The 2026 review reports a small heterogeneous behavioral effect and weaker evidence for delayed or repeated behavior. Expertise, trustworthiness, legitimate power/status, obedience, fame, and message quality must remain separate; source relevance and conflict disclosure are necessary local conditions.

## M27 — Scarcity cues (B)

- Barton, Zlatevska & Oppewal (2022), product-scarcity meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretai.2022.06.003).
- Ladeira et al. (2023), product-scarcity meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/mar.21816).

Scarcity effects are heterogeneous by source of scarcity, product, and outcome. The literature often uses intention or decision proxies; do not turn it into a completed-purchase forecast, and never fabricate stock or a deadline.

## N12 — Normative Social Influence (B)

- Deutsch & Gerard (1955), normative versus informational social influence, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0046408).
- Bond & Smith (1996), Asch-paradigm conformity meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.119.1.111).
- Melnyk, van Herpen & Trijp (2013), consumer social-norm meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1027/2151-2604/a000352).

An injunctive message is not proof of an approval/sanction motive. Evidence varies with public visibility, sanctions, group relevance, culture, and reactance; public compliance must be separated from private belief.

## N13 — Informational Social Influence (B)

- Deutsch & Gerard (1955), normative versus informational social influence, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0046408).
- Melnyk, van Herpen & Trijp (2013), consumer social-norm meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1027/2151-2604/a000352).

The foundational distinction is strong, but a dedicated modern pooled estimate for private informational updating is not established here. Source independence, diagnosticity, uncertainty, and the distinction between belief and public response limit transportability; correlated or unproven reviews are not independent evidence.
