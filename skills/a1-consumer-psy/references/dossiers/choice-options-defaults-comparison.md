# Choice: options, defaults, and comparison model dossiers

These model dossiers cover current-state choices, preselection, choice complexity, reference-sensitive comparison, numeric references, asymmetric dominance, and framing. They are diagnosis tools, not labels for any plan card, number, or interface change. Each model section owns its evidence grade, sources, and evidence limitation; none predicts a local effect by itself.

<a id="m11"></a>

## M11 — Status-quo bias

- **Mechanism:** a person disproportionately retains the current state or prior choice.
- **Diagnostic signals:** an explicit current choice exists; switching requires action; stable users retain an existing option more than comparable new users.
- **Distinguish from:** a default (preselected option), switching friction, inertia, endowment, sunk cost, or omission. A non-switch is not automatically status-quo bias.
- **Useful test:** compare retention or switching under a transparent, equal-effort choice versus the present flow; segment current versus new users. A similar result with no current state weakens this explanation.
- **Applicability limit:** do not call every low-switching rate a cognitive bias.

### Evidence (B)

- Samuelson & Zeckhauser (1988), *Status quo bias in decision making*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00055564).
- Xiao et al. (2021), direct replication, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.15626/MP.2020.2470).
- Godefroid et al. (2023), measurement review, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11301-022-00283-8).

Use only with a genuine current-state comparison; nearby mechanisms often confound it.

## M12 — Default effect

- **Mechanism:** an option is selected more often when it is preselected or applied automatically while opting out remains possible.
- **Diagnostic signals:** a real preselection or automatic enrollment exists; users can change it; selection differs from an otherwise comparable active-choice flow.
- **Distinguish from:** status quo, recommendation, visual salience, forced choice, and hidden consent. No preselection means this card is not the primary explanation.
- **Useful test:** compare transparent active choice and transparent default variants; track opt-out, downstream satisfaction, cancellation, and complaints. No differential selection weakens the hypothesis.
- **Applicability limit:** defaults can signal endorsement or reduce effort; they are not a license to hide choices.

### Evidence (A)

- Jachimowicz et al. (2019), meta-analysis across 58 datasets / 55 studies / 35 articles, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/bpp.2018.43).

The meta-analysis reports a positive average default effect with high heterogeneity. Do not convert its aggregate estimate into a local forecast.

## M13 — Choice overload

- **Mechanism:** a choice set can impair decision or satisfaction when its complexity exceeds a user's ability or motivation to evaluate it.
- **Diagnostic signals:** the change increased meaningful alternatives, attributes, comparability burden, or decision stakes; users delay, abandon, or choose poorly relative to a simpler, informative alternative.
- **Distinguish from:** generic more-options-is-bad stories, poor information hierarchy, unsuitable options, traffic mismatch, or a decoy/anchor. There is no universal option-count threshold.
- **Useful test:** hold option quality and truthfulness constant while reducing complexity or adding a transparent decision aid; track completion plus decision quality and regret. No improvement weakens the hypothesis.
- **Applicability limit:** there is no universal option-count threshold; moderator conditions matter.

### Evidence (B)

- Scheibehenne, Greifeneder & Todd (2010), meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/651235).
- Chernev, Böckenholt & Goodman (2015), meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2014.08.002), with [corrigendum](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2015.07.001).

There is no universal threshold; the 2010 synthesis found a near-zero average effect amid high heterogeneity.

## M30 — Loss aversion

- **Mechanism:** comparable losses can receive more decision weight than gains; this is one component of prospect theory, not a synonym for any reluctance to pay or change.
- **Diagnostic signals:** equivalent gain-versus-loss descriptions lead to different choices while the factual outcome, stakes, and timing are held constant.
- **Distinguish from:** endowment, status quo, risk aversion, switching cost, and bad offer economics.
- **Useful test:** compare factually equivalent, non-deceptive gain and loss frames; measure conversion, comprehension, complaints, cancellation, and later satisfaction. No framing difference weakens the hypothesis.
- **Applicability limit:** do not assume a fixed loss-to-gain ratio.

### Evidence (C)

- Kahneman & Tversky (1979), prospect theory, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185).
- Ruggeri et al. (2020), large-scale evidence, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-020-0886-x).
- Brown et al. (2024), review, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.20221698).

Avoid fixed “losses are twice gains” claims; magnitude and interpretation remain contested.

## M31 — Anchoring

- **Mechanism:** an initial relevant numeric reference can shift later numerical judgment or willingness to pay.
- **Diagnostic signals:** users see an earlier number before estimating, comparing, or choosing; comparable groups receive different credible references; later judgments shift toward the reference.
- **Distinguish from:** an informative market price, a transparent comparison, decoy effects, or framing. A number merely appearing on a page is not proof of anchoring.
- **Useful test:** randomize two truthful, relevant reference presentations while holding the offer constant; track choice, value perception, refunds, and trust. No directional movement weakens the hypothesis.
- **Applicability limit:** an arbitrary or incidental number may not be a meaningful anchor.

### Evidence (A)

- Tversky & Kahneman (1974), foundational study, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124).
- Schley & Weingarten (2026), meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.03238).
- Simonson & Drolet (2004), consumer willingness-to-pay, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/425103).

Evidence supports anchoring, while estimates vary greatly and arbitrary anchors can be weaker.

<a id="m32"></a>

## M32 — Decoy effect

- **Mechanism:** adding an asymmetrically dominated third option can alter preference between two target options.
- **Diagnostic signals:** a binary choice becomes a ternary set; the added option is genuinely worse than one target on all relevant dimensions and comparable to it; target shares are measured.
- **Distinguish from:** a merely unattractive plan, a transparent lower-value tier, anchor, or choice overload. Without asymmetric dominance, do not use this model.
- **Useful test:** compare the two-target set with and without the clearly labeled third option; track target share, comprehension, regret, and cancellation. No targeted share shift weakens the hypothesis.
- **Applicability limit:** do not treat an added plan as a decoy without asymmetric dominance.

### Evidence (B)

- Huber, Payne & Puto (1982), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/208899).
- Dumbalska et al. (2020), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2005058117).
- Trendl et al. (2021), naturalistic zero-effect study, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/dec0000145).

Transport to ordinary product settings is uncertain; verify asymmetric dominance and test locally.

<a id="m33"></a>

## M33 — Framing

- **Mechanism:** equivalent descriptions or reference points can lead to different choices because people interpret the decision differently.
- **Diagnostic signals:** alternatives are materially equivalent; only the presentation of outcomes, risk, or reference point changes; comprehension is preserved.
- **Distinguish from:** changed economics, omitted terms, loss aversion, anchoring, and manipulation through ambiguity. If the meaning changes, this is not a clean framing test.
- **Useful test:** test matched, plain-language frames with equal facts and disclosure; compare selection, comprehension, confidence, and downstream outcomes. A difference without comprehension preservation is not a clean win.
- **Applicability limit:** framing is a family of effects, not one universal mechanism.

### Evidence (A)

- Tversky & Kahneman (1981), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7455683).
- Kühberger (1998), meta-analysis, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1006/obhd.1998.2781).

Framing effects have documented boundary conditions; maintain factual equivalence and check comprehension.
