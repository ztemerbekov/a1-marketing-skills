# Article Source Policy

This file owns the content boundary for `a1-article-writing`.

## Allowed Sources

Use only:

1. Facts, claims, examples, constraints, and source links explicitly supplied in the current request.
2. Notes, interviews, transcripts, research, documents, and datasets the user selected for the article.
3. Clearly applicable facts supplied earlier in the conversation.
4. The first eligible repository Marketing Context selected by `SKILL.md`.
5. External sources gathered for this article when the user requests research or when current factual verification is necessary and the environment provides an appropriate research tool.

Treat a user's desired conclusion, tone, or positioning as a writing instruction, not evidence that the conclusion is true.

## Source Map

Before outlining, distinguish:

- verified facts and observations;
- firsthand experience and its limits;
- attributed expert statements;
- quotations that must remain exact;
- interpretations or recommendations supported by those inputs;
- claims that still need evidence;
- editorial preferences rather than facts.

Every name, number, date, quotation, causal claim, result, comparison, and specific example in the article must trace to the source map.

## Research and Verification

Completed research is valid input. When new research is part of the accepted article job, use sources appropriate to the claim and keep links close enough for the user to verify them. Prefer primary sources for factual, technical, scientific, legal, financial, and product claims.

Do not use plausible model knowledge as silent evidence. If a claim could have changed or is material to the article's credibility, verify it or mark the gap.

When the environment cannot perform requested research, produce the strongest honest outline or partial draft the sources support and name what must be verified. Do not fill the gap with generic prose.

## Interviews, Experience, and Quotations

An interview is a source, not automatic proof. Attribute personal claims and flag statements that require independent verification.

Present firsthand experience at its actual scale. One person's case is one case unless broader evidence supports generalization. Preserve conditions that may have affected the result.

Keep supplied quotations exact apart from user-authorized cleanup. Never invent a quotation or attribute draft language as something a person already said. A proposed quotation must be labeled for approval.

## Marketing Context

Treat audience, Brand Voice, and business-goal entries as defaults that a current request may override. Treat product facts, evidence, and explicit prohibitions as protected inputs.

When a current instruction conflicts with a protected input, preserve the protected fact and surface the conflict. Ask one concise question only when every credible article would require choosing which protected fact is true.

## Missing Material

Missing optional context does not block useful work. Proceed when a credible source-supported result is possible.

When the article's central promise lacks support, do one of the following:

- in Outline mode, provide an evidence plan;
- in Draft mode, ask for the one blocking source when no honest draft is possible;
- when a partial draft remains useful, write only supported sections and mark unresolved gaps outside the article.

Do not hide factual gaps inside smooth generalities or publishable-sounding placeholders.
