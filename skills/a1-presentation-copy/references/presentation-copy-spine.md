# Presentation-Copy Spine

Every completed run follows these stages in order.

## 1. Classify the Presentation Job

Identify the primary audience, occasion, delivery mode, decision or understanding required, next step, and output mode. A live talk may rely on the speaker; a standalone deck must carry more context on the slides.

Ask one concise question only when the audience, occasion, or required next step would otherwise be chosen arbitrarily.

**Complete when:** the presentation has one audience situation and one observable job.

## 2. Build the Source Map

Apply [source-policy.md](source-policy.md). Separate conclusions, supporting facts, data, examples, objections, decisions, visual assets, and source gaps.

**Complete when:** every proposed takeaway has support or is marked as a gap.

## 3. Set the Message Path

Choose the shortest sequence that moves the audience from its starting knowledge to the required conclusion, decision, or action. Organize by audience questions and dependencies rather than by the order in which the source material arrived.

Remove background that does not change interpretation or action. Keep reference detail after the main path or in an appendix when the audience may need to inspect it.

**Complete when:** each slide has a distinct reason to exist and the sequence makes the conclusion earned.

## 4. Design the Slide Units

Give each slide one takeaway. Use a headline that states that takeaway rather than naming a broad topic. Decide what evidence should be shown: a chart, table, screenshot, diagram, photograph, quotation, number, example, or comparison.

Use on-slide text to orient the audience and interpret the evidence. Keep explanation in speaker notes only when the delivery mode supports it.

**Complete when:** every slide can be understood as one claim supported by appropriate material.

## 5. Draft and Test

Write the smallest amount of copy that preserves the intended meaning. Keep lists grammatically parallel and separate evidence from commentary. End with the actual decision, action, owner, contact, or next step supported by the sources.

Revise until:

- headlines reveal the presentation's argument when read in sequence;
- no slide combines unrelated takeaways;
- every number, quotation, example, and conclusion is traceable;
- charts and visuals have an explicit audience-facing purpose;
- live and standalone reading requirements are handled correctly;
- the ending completes the presentation job without a ceremonial summary.

**Complete when:** the deck can guide the intended audience without unsupported claims or presenter-dependent gaps.
