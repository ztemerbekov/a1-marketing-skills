# Press-Release Source Policy

This file owns the content boundary for `a1-press-release`.

## Allowed Sources

Use only:

1. Event, organization, product, participant, timing, location, and action facts explicitly supplied in the current request, with their confirmation status preserved.
2. Briefs, fact sheets, approved quotations, research summaries, schedules, registration pages, media kits, and background material selected by the user.
3. Clearly applicable facts supplied earlier in the conversation.
4. The first eligible repository Marketing Context selected by `SKILL.md`.

Treat the user's wish for a strong headline or broad coverage as editorial direction, not evidence that an event is unique, historic, first, largest, or newsworthy to every audience.

## News Map

Separate the sources into:

- the concrete event or change;
- who is acting and who is affected;
- date, time, place, or availability;
- why the event matters to the intended media audience;
- verified facts and mechanisms;
- participation, registration, access, or follow-up action;
- approved quotation and speaker attribution;
- organization boilerplate;
- media contact and asset links;
- permissions or facts still awaiting verification.

Every name, title, date, number, superlative, quotation, link, partnership, result, comparison, and background claim in the release must trace to this map.

## Readiness Map

Classify each distribution-critical input as:

- **Confirmed:** the supplied source establishes the fact and the user represents it as current for this release;
- **Approved:** confirmed wording, attribution, partnership language, confidential detail, or another item whose source records the required internal permission;
- **Unresolved:** conflicting, incomplete, draft, permission-pending, or otherwise not safe to present as distribution-ready.

Public verification can confirm public facts. It cannot grant internal approval or resolve confidential and contractual authority.

Treat the release as distribution-ready only when every critical input is confirmed and every required permission is approved. Otherwise keep it as a working draft.

## Verification

Do not silently correct or complete the announcement from model knowledge. Check source consistency for dates, weekday, time zone, location, spelling, titles, organization names, prices, eligibility, deadlines, and links.

External verification may confirm a public fact when the user requests it and the environment provides an appropriate tool. It does not replace internal approval for confidential, contractual, partnership, financial, legal, or forward-looking information.

Keep unresolved facts outside authoritative prose. List them separately for a working draft; omit them from distribution-ready copy.

## Quotations

Use a quotation only when the source provides approved words or the user explicitly asks for proposed wording.

Never present model-written language as something a person already said. Make its proposed status and need for approval unmistakable until the user confirms approval.

An approved quote should add judgment, explanation, commitment, or a human consequence. A ceremonial quote may be omitted when it repeats the lead without adding information.

## Claims and Partners

Preserve claim qualifications and the actual role of programs, funders, sponsors, and partners. Explain what a partnership or program changed only when the source establishes the contribution.

Do not add an organization, endorsement, customer, market position, award, or comparative claim because it would make the release sound more important.

## Marketing Context

Use approved product and organization facts, claims and evidence, voice defaults, and explicit prohibitions from the selected Marketing Context. Current event facts must still come from the current release sources.

When a current request conflicts with a protected fact, preserve the protected input and surface the conflict. Ask one concise question only when every accurate release would require choosing which approved fact is current.
