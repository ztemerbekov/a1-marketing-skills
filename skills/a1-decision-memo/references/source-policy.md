# Decision-Memo Source Policy

This file owns the content boundary for `a1-decision-memo`.

## Allowed Sources

Use only:

1. The current instruction and facts explicitly supplied with it.
2. Notes, reports, correspondence, analysis, tables, plans, or other material the user selected for the memo.
3. Clearly applicable facts supplied earlier in the conversation.
4. The first eligible repository Marketing Context selected by `SKILL.md`.

Do not silently complete specialist analysis from model knowledge. A supplied legal opinion, financial calculation, technical assessment, research report, or project plan is completed external input; preserve its meaning and attribution rather than re-performing the underlying profession.

## Decision Map

Separate the sources into:

- current status and material background;
- decisions already made;
- the decision, approval, response, or awareness requested now;
- options explicitly present in the sources;
- criteria and tradeoffs supplied for those options;
- recommendations and who made them;
- risks, dependencies, unknowns, owners, and deadlines;
- evidence and detail that support the summary.

Every fact, recommendation, option, deadline, owner, figure, quotation, and causal explanation in the memo must trace to this map.

## Recommendations and Decisions

Do not turn the editor's preferred wording into a substantive recommendation. A memo may foreground a recommendation already present in the sources or derive a narrow conclusion when the supplied criteria make it explicit and no material judgment is added.

When options exist but the sources do not establish how to choose, present the options and the unresolved criterion. Do not select one merely to make the memo decisive.

Distinguish clearly among:

- a fact established by the sources;
- an author's or expert's assessment;
- a proposed option;
- a recommendation;
- a decision already made;
- an action awaiting approval.

## Marketing Context

Use audience, Brand Voice, and business-goal entries only as defaults when clearly relevant to the memo. Preserve product facts, evidence, and explicit prohibitions as protected inputs.

A current instruction may override style defaults but not silently replace protected facts. Surface a conflict, and ask one concise question only when every accurate memo would require choosing which protected fact is true.

## Source Gaps

Missing optional context does not block a useful memo. Proceed when the current state and the decision, response, or awareness job can be represented honestly.

Name a gap when it affects the decision: a missing owner, deadline, amount, approval authority, option, criterion, risk, or source for a central claim. Keep gaps outside the finished memo unless the uncertainty itself belongs in the decision record.

Do not invent administrative details to make the memo look complete.
