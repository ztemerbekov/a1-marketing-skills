# Tone and Depth

Load this reference only when the selected operation or source defect involves tone, empathy, examples, explanatory depth, or a text that feels generic despite clean sentences.

## Tone Is a Relationship

Tone is shaped by what the writer chooses to discuss, how they regard the reader and subject, the degree of empathy, the evidence they bring, and the channel in which the text appears.

Do not treat tone as a list of friendly words, jokes, exclamation marks, or branded phrases. Those surface markers cannot repair a condescending, evasive, or unsupported message.

Default to direct peer communication. Adjust only from the source, current instruction, or eligible Marketing Context.

## Stance and Empathy

Check whether the text:

- explains behavior instead of judging the reader;
- acknowledges real constraints relevant to the source;
- speaks plainly about the writer's intent;
- avoids forced intimacy with strangers;
- keeps authority proportional to evidence and experience;
- preserves respectful disagreement and uncertainty.

Replace moral pressure with a supported consequence or practical option. Do not invent hardship, reassurance, or empathy that the sources do not establish.

## Authentic Voice

Preserve meaningful first person, humor, rhythm, emotion, and firsthand detail. Remove performance that makes the writer sound like a synthetic persona or a different social role without source support.

A distinctive voice may be formal, restrained, playful, technical, or blunt. The test is whether it remains credible and useful in the reader relationship, not whether it matches a universal friendly style.

## Depth Through Resolution

A text becomes deeper when it helps the reader recognize and use an idea. Source-supported depth may come from:

- a concrete example or counterexample;
- the mechanism behind a claim;
- how to implement advice;
- conditions and exceptions;
- costs and tradeoffs;
- a realistic comparison;
- a sequence of decisions and consequences;
- detail calibrated to the reader's knowledge.

Do not lengthen the text by restating the same abstraction. Add depth only from allowed sources.

## Expert Compression

Experts often present the conclusion and omit the experience that made it obvious. When the sources contain that path, restore enough examples, intermediate reasoning, or implementation detail for the intended reader.

Do not over-explain familiar terms to an expert audience. Depth is relative to the reader and task.

## Advice

Useful advice often needs three layers:

1. what to do;
2. why it matters or works;
3. how to apply it under realistic conditions.

Keep only the layers the sources support. If the source gives a slogan but no implementation, do not fabricate steps; surface the limitation in a review or keep the claim appropriately narrow in an edit.

## Completion Check

Tone-and-depth work is complete when the text has a credible reader relationship, preserves authentic voice, and provides the source-supported examples, mechanisms, application, or limits needed to make its ideas usable.
