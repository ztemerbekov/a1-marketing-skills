# Sentence Level

At sentence level, make actors, actions, objects, conditions, and relationships easy to recover.

## Grammar, Government, and Collocation

An inferable meaning does not excuse incorrect or unnatural language. Check:

- agreement, tense, person, case, and number;
- verb, noun, adjective, and preposition government;
- lexical collocation in the intended meaning;
- pronoun reference and parallel construction;
- translation-shaped syntax or a mechanical calque.

Correct the smallest unit that restores natural meaning. When two repairs express materially different meanings and the source cannot choose, apply the significant-ambiguity rule in [source-boundary.md](source-boundary.md).

Do not classify a professional term, regional usage, conversational construction, or intentional voice as an error merely because a more formal alternative exists.

## Actors and Actions

Dense prose often hides action inside abstract nouns or passive constructions: `implementation was conducted`, `processing is carried out`, `производится обработка`, `осуществляется реализация`.

When the source establishes the actor:

- name who acts;
- turn nominalized actions into verbs;
- show what changes and for whom;
- keep the action close to its subject.

When the actor is unknown or deliberately irrelevant, simplify without inventing one.

## Overload

A sentence is overloaded when several claims, conditions, exceptions, or nested interruptions compete for attention.

Useful moves include:

- split independent claims;
- place a material condition before the outcome it controls;
- move explanation after the main action;
- turn a long parenthetical into a separate sentence;
- remove repetition while preserving qualifications.

One sentence may contain several clauses when they form one coherent relationship. Do not shorten mechanically.

## Word Order

Keep the subject and governing action visible early enough. Avoid inserting long qualifications between an actor and its action. Put familiar context before new information when that improves flow, but move a time-sensitive answer or decision forward when the reader needs it first.

## Noun and Genitive Chains

Chains such as `quality control of contractor employee work execution` or `контроль качества выполнения работ сотрудников подрядчика` obscure relationships.

Unpack them through verbs, shorter noun groups, and explicit links: who controls what, whose work, for whom, when, and why.

## Bureaucratic Syntax

Official tone does not require fog. Simplify inflated prepositional phrases, passive constructions, and nominalizations while preserving legal or institutional precision.

Replace a passive with an active construction when the actor is known and relevant. Keep the passive when the action or result legitimately matters more than the actor.

## Hedging

Preserve uncertainty that limits a claim. Remove duplicated hedging such as `may possibly`, `could potentially`, or `возможно, может`, leaving the most accurate marker.

Never remove a hedge while keeping the same tentative outcome as a categorical promise.

## Mental Picture

Prefer source-supported objects, actions, time, conditions, and consequences over abstraction. A reader should be able to identify what happens, not merely that a process or improvement exists.

Do not invent a scene, actor, measurement, or example to make the sentence concrete.

## Completion Check

Sentence-level work is complete when grammar and collocation are natural, relationships are unambiguous, qualifications remain accurate, and complexity reflects the idea rather than the wording.
