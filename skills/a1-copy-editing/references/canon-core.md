# Canon Core

This compact principle set guides editing quality after the operation and source boundary are fixed. It does not redefine either contract.

## Core Principles

- **Meaning before polish.** Preserve the source-supported intention unless the user asks to change it.
- **Reader before author.** Help the reader understand, decide, verify, or act instead of foregrounding the writer's effort or status.
- **Shorter only when denser.** Remove expendable material, not useful facts, qualifications, examples, or voice.
- **Clear before impressive.** Prefer language the intended reader can interpret without decoding.
- **Concrete when supported.** Show people, actions, objects, conditions, and consequences available in the allowed sources.
- **Evidence before evaluation.** A sentence should not sound more certain, important, or persuasive than its support allows.
- **Idiomatic prose.** Correct grammar, government, collocation, reference, and translation-shaped phrasing without flattening legitimate voice.
- **Structure serves the job.** Put information where the reader needs it rather than preserving chronology or a familiar template by default.
- **Tone is a relationship.** Preserve a credible stance toward the reader; friendliness, authority, or energy cannot be manufactured by decorative wording alone.
- **Useful depth.** Keep or surface examples, mechanisms, implementation detail, tradeoffs, and limits when they help the reader use the idea.
