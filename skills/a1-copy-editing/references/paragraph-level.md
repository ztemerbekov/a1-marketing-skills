# Paragraph Level

A paragraph should move the reader through one main idea without forcing them to assemble it from unrelated detail.

## One Main Idea

State the paragraph's job in one short sentence. If the paragraph simultaneously explains the problem, product, price, company history, and next action, split or regroup it.

Keep necessary support with the idea it explains. Do not split a qualification or counterexample so far from its claim that the meaning changes.

## Informative Opening

Put the subject, action, condition, or conclusion near the start. Remove setup that postpones the topic.

Weak openings include `It is important to note`, `In today's world`, `At the present time`, `Следует отметить`, and `На сегодняшний день` when the following sentence carries the real information.

The opening sentence should orient the reader to the paragraph's main move. Put chronology or explanation after the point when that order is easier to use.

## Self-Containment

A paragraph should remain understandable without unnecessary backtracking.

- Replace an ambiguous pronoun with the specific subject when the source establishes it.
- Repeat a key term when repetition is clearer than a vague `this`, `they`, `это`, or `они`.
- Remove transitions that signal continuity without naming the connection.
- Preserve intentional cross-reference when the document depends on it.

## Logic and Sequence

Reduce mental jumps:

- keep cause and consequence in a usable order;
- group related evidence;
- separate conditions from outcomes;
- distinguish current state, history, and next action;
- provide a concrete bridge when the relationship is not obvious.

Remove a formulaic conclusion such as `Thus, we have considered` or `Таким образом, мы рассмотрели` when it adds no new implication.

## Questions

A question should organize a real reader concern or create useful rhythm and receive an answer. Convert decorative questions into statements when they merely imitate curiosity.

Do not add a new question that the source cannot answer.

## Lists

Use a list when items are genuinely parallel and scanning matters.

Check that items:

- answer the same question;
- use comparable grammatical form;
- carry similar levels of detail;
- preserve dependencies and qualifications;
- avoid unnecessary nesting.

Use paragraphs for reasoning, chronology, or a story. A visual list should represent a semantic list.

## Completion Check

Paragraph-level work is complete when each paragraph has one recoverable idea, starts where the reader needs it, and uses prose or a list that matches the material's logic.
