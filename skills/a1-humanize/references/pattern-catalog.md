# AI-Writing Pattern Catalog

This is a compact, language-independent adaptation of Humanizer’s 35 patterns. Search for equivalent functions, including Russian ones, rather than literal English strings.

1. inflated significance, legacy, or broader trends;
2. notability name-dropping;
3. superficial participial analysis;
4. promotional or advertising language;
5. vague attribution or weasel words;
6. formulaic challenge-and-future sections;
7. clustered AI vocabulary (for example, “pivotal,” “landscape,” “quietly,” “ключевой,” “динамичный”); treat “gate,” “gated,” and “gating” as signals only when figurative, and preserve established technical usage;
8. copula avoidance such as “serves as” or “boasts” instead of direct verbs;
9. negative parallelism and tailing negations, including «не просто X, а Y»;
10. forced rule-of-three lists;
11. synonym cycling for one concept or several consecutive sentences with the same unhelpful opening; vary by ear without removing deliberate repetition that creates rhythm or emphasis;
12. false rhetorical ranges;
13. passive or subjectless fragments that obscure a useful actor;
14. em and en dashes; replace them unless the voice sample establishes them;
15. mechanical bolding;
16. repetitive inline-header lists;
17. title-case headings outside the target language’s normal convention;
18. decorative emojis that carry no meaning or voice;
19. curly quotation marks when the target format requires straight quotes;
20. chatbot greetings, closers, and offers to continue;
21. knowledge-cutoff disclaimers and speculative gap-filling;
22. sycophantic praise and automatic agreement;
23. filler phrases such as “in order to” and local equivalents;
24. stacked hedging that exceeds the source’s uncertainty;
25. generic positive conclusions;
26. mechanical over-hyphenation;
27. persuasive authority tropes such as “at its core”;
28. formal or casual signposting that announces the next point instead of stating it, such as “let’s dive in,” “heads up,” “quick note,” “before I forget,” or «давайте разберёмся»;
29. a heading followed by its rhetorical restatement;
30. diff-anchored writing outside changelogs, release notes, and migration guides;
31. manufactured punchlines and staccato drama;
32. aphorism formulas that substitute for a concrete claim; and
33. theatrical conversational openers such as “Honestly?” or «если честно»;
34. answering objections no one raised (“shadowboxing”): an unattributed defense against an objection that never appears in the text; remove the defensive detour, but keep real scope, safety or legal notices, named objections, replies, FAQs, and claims that the text genuinely uses; and
35. rejecting fake alternatives (“editorial scar tissue”): an implausible option introduced only to be rejected and never used again; remove the discarded option, preserve any real constraint it contains, and keep alternatives that a reader could reasonably consider.

## False-Positive Guard

Do not rewrite text merely because it is polished, formal, dry, grammatical, or well formatted, or because it uses one catalog pattern. Preserve specific unusual details, real mixed feelings already in the source, dated references, deliberate first-person choices, authentic asides, deliberate repeated openings, useful limits and disclaimers, attributed or fully engaged objections, real alternatives, salutations, sign-offs, quotations, titles, proper names, and examples that mention rather than use a pattern. When in doubt, look for clusters and ask whether the change improves this text.
