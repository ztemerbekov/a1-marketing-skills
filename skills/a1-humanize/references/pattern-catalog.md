# AI-Writing Pattern Catalog

This is a compact, language-independent adaptation of Humanizer’s 33 patterns. Search for equivalent functions, including Russian ones, rather than literal English strings.

1. inflated significance, legacy, or broader trends;
2. notability name-dropping;
3. superficial participial analysis;
4. promotional or advertising language;
5. vague attribution or weasel words;
6. formulaic challenge-and-future sections;
7. clustered AI vocabulary (for example, “pivotal,” “landscape,” “ключевой,” “динамичный”);
8. copula avoidance such as “serves as” or “boasts” instead of direct verbs;
9. negative parallelism and tailing negations, including «не просто X, а Y»;
10. forced rule-of-three lists;
11. synonym cycling for one concept;
12. false rhetorical ranges;
13. passive or subjectless fragments that obscure a useful actor;
14. em and en dashes; replace them unless the voice sample establishes them;
15. mechanical bolding;
16. repetitive inline-header lists;
17. title-case headings outside the target language’s normal convention;
18. decorative emojis that carry no meaning or voice;
19. curly quotation marks when the target format requires straight quotes;
20. chatbot greetings, closers, and offers to continue;
21. knowledge-cutoff disclaimers and speculative gap-filling;
22. sycophantic praise and automatic agreement;
23. filler phrases such as “in order to” and local equivalents;
24. stacked hedging that exceeds the source’s uncertainty;
25. generic positive conclusions;
26. mechanical over-hyphenation;
27. persuasive authority tropes such as “at its core”;
28. signposting such as “let’s dive in” or «давайте разберёмся»;
29. a heading followed by its rhetorical restatement;
30. diff-anchored writing outside changelogs, release notes, and migration guides;
31. manufactured punchlines and staccato drama;
32. aphorism formulas that substitute for a concrete claim; and
33. theatrical conversational openers such as “Honestly?” or «если честно».

## False-Positive Guard

Do not rewrite text merely because it is polished, formal, dry, grammatical, or well formatted, or because it uses one catalog pattern. Preserve specific unusual details, real mixed feelings already in the source, dated references, deliberate first-person choices, authentic asides, salutations, sign-offs, quotations, titles, proper names, and examples that mention rather than use a pattern. When in doubt, look for clusters and ask whether the change improves this text.
