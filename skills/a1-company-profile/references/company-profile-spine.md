# Company-Profile Spine

Every completed run follows these stages in order.

## 1. Classify the Profile Job

Identify the primary audience context, where the profile will appear, what readers need to understand, and whether the output is an Outline or Draft. A general About page may serve several readers, but one primary context must govern emphasis and depth.

Ask one concise question only when the audience context or publication surface would materially change the company story.

**Complete when:** the profile has one public use and one primary reader context.

## 2. Build the Company Fact Map

Apply [source-policy.md](source-policy.md). Separate company category, customers, work, operating model, geography, history, proof, policies, people, mission, values, limitations, and contact or next-step information.

**Complete when:** each possible company statement is traceable and unsupported corporate claims are excluded.

## 3. Answer Reader Questions

Choose sections from actual reader questions, such as:

- What does the company do?
- Who does it serve?
- How does its work or product operate?
- Where and under what conditions does it work?
- Why is the company credible?
- What history, people, policies, or principles materially explain its behavior?
- What should the reader do next?

Do not force a conventional history, mission, team, or values section when it adds no useful supported answer.

**Complete when:** every section helps the intended reader understand or evaluate the company.

## 4. Ground Mission and Values

Use an approved mission or value only when the sources provide its actual wording or observable company decisions, practices, exclusions, investments, policies, or tradeoffs. Explain those behaviors instead of praising the company with abstract virtues.

**Complete when:** any mission or value is visible in what the company does, not merely what it calls itself.

## 5. Draft and Test

Lead with a plain-language category and the company's work. Put chronology, legal form, awards, investor information, downloads, and reference detail where readers need them rather than automatically at the opening.

Revise until:

- readers can identify what the company does and for whom;
- specific claims, dates, names, credentials, and results are sourced;
- mission and values are supported by observable behavior;
- company history has relevance rather than ceremonial length;
- the profile distinguishes company information from an offer-specific sales page;
- the ending provides an accurate next step or stops when the reader's questions are answered.

**Complete when:** the profile is informative, credible, and usable without invented identity or generic corporate language.
