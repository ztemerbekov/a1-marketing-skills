# Marketing decision lenses

Use these lenses to scan a destination breadth-first. They are prompts for finding decisions, not a mandatory checklist. Select only lenses that can materially change the route.

| Lens | Look for decisions about |
|---|---|
| Outcome | Business result, marketing objective, success measure, time horizon |
| Market | Category, geography, demand, alternatives, competitive frame |
| Audience | Segment, priority customer, buying situation, decision-makers |
| Problem | Customer progress, unmet need, barrier, urgency, evidence of demand |
| Value | Offer, benefit, differentiation, proof, price or exchange |
| Position | Category choice, frame of reference, promise, reasons to believe |
| Journey | Discovery, evaluation, conversion, onboarding, retention |
| Channel | Reach, distribution, media role, channel constraints, ownership |
| Expression | Message, creative direction, voice, format, required adaptation |
| Economics | Budget, capacity, unit economics, opportunity cost |
| Measurement | Baseline, leading signal, outcome metric, countermetric, decision rule |
| Risk | Legal, ethical, reputational, operational, dependency, reversibility |

## Selection rule

Start with the Destination and ask which lenses could change a consequential choice. Skip lenses already settled by confirmed sources and lenses outside the destination. Phrase each resulting item as a decision question, not a production task.

Prefer questions that expose a trade-off. For example:

- `Which customer segment should lead the launch?`
- `What must the offer promise, and what must it avoid promising?`
- `Which channel deserves the first constrained test?`
- `What result would justify continuing, changing, or stopping?`

Avoid task-shaped entries such as `make a landing page`, `launch ads`, or `write ten posts`. Convert them to the unresolved choice that must precede execution, or leave them for the downstream plan.
